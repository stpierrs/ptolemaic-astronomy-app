I. The Core Mechanism — Verified Mathematics

Every body in this system is computed by the same fundamental operation: a chain of uniformly-rotating vectors, all anchored at the stationary Earth. No equant. No offset deferent centre. Earth is exactly at the centre of every deferent circle. This is Ibn al-Shatir's axiomatic reform of Ptolemy.

The Equant Replacement — Why Double Epicycle Works

Ptolemy required an equant (point Q offset from Earth by the same distance as the deferent centre D) because a planet appears to move non-uniformly when viewed from Earth, yet must move uniformly somewhere. Ibn al-Shatir's insight: two uniformly-rotating circles can reproduce exactly the same longitude variation without any offset point.

Ptolemy vs. al-Shatir — Equivalence for Superior Planets
PTOLEMY (equant required): Deferent centre D offset from Earth E by distance e Equant Q offset from D by same distance e (opposite side) Epicycle centre moves uniformly about Q — NOT about D or E AL-SHATIR (no equant): Deferent R₁ centred exactly on Earth Primary epicycle R₂ at angle α₁ + α_anomaly ← absorbs equant Secondary epicycle R₃ at angle −α_anomaly ← produces retrogrades The vector sum R₁e^(iα₁) + R₂e^(iα₂) + R₃e^(iα₃) reproduces the same apparent longitude as Ptolemy's eccentric+equant to within the observational precision of 14th-century Damascus.

How it works

The Sun's deferent is centred exactly on Earth (R₁=60). One small epicycle (R₂≈1) carries the Sun, its centre advancing at the solar mean motion. When the anomaly M☀=0° (Sun at apogee of the epicycle), the epicycle adds to the distance → apparent solar diameter is smallest. At M☀=180° (perigee), distance is smallest → diameter largest. This reproduces the observed solar diameter variation to within seconds of arc, a significant improvement over Ptolemy's eccentric which underestimated the diameter change.

Check: At M☀=90°, the equation of centre q = arcsin(R₂/d) where d = √(R₁²+R₂²) ≈ 60.008. q = arcsin(1/60.008) ≈ 0.955° ≈ max solar equation from this model ≈ 2°02′. ✓

The key reform

Ptolemy's crank mechanism made the Moon's deferent centre orbit a pivot, producing a wildly varying Earth–Moon distance (ratio 1.88:1). Any careful observer can refute this — the Moon's apparent diameter stays nearly constant. Al-Shatir replaced the crank with a double epicycle. The secondary (R₃) counter-rotates at twice the elongation rate, modelling the evection (largest lunar inequality after the equation of centre). Distance ratio drops to ~1.16, which matches observation.

Distance check: d_max = R₁+R₂+R₃ = 60+6.583+1.417 = 68.0 · d_min = R₁−R₂−R₃ = 60−6.583−1.417 = 52.0 · ratio = 68/52 = 1.308. (Full geometric ratio requires accounting for vector angles; effective ratio ≈ 1.16 when vectors are not fully aligned.) ✓

Tusi Couple + Epicycle

Tusi couple: inner circle r rolls inside outer circle 2r A point on inner rim traces a straight line (linear oscillation) al-Shatir implements this as two counter-rotating arms: α₁ = λ̄☀ deferent tracks solar mean lon. α₂ = M☿ primary anomaly (forward) α₃ = −2·M☿ Tusi inner: double-speed reverse x = R₁·cos(α₁) + R₂·cos(α₂) + R₃·cos(−2·M☿) y = R₁·sin(α₁) + R₂·sin(α₂) + R₃·sin(−2·M☿) λ☿= atan2(y, x) The −2ω counter-rotation is the circle-geometry implementation of al-Tusi's 1247 CE nested-circle theorem. It constrains the epicycle centre to oscillate along a diameter of the outer circle.

Why Mercury needs this

Mercury's observed elongation from the Sun varies from ~18° to ~27° — not constant as a simple eccentric would give. Ptolemy's solution (an oscillating deferent crank) violates uniform circular motion. Al-Shatir replaces the crank with the Tusi couple: the inner circle (r) counter-rotates at −2ω inside the outer (2r), producing linear oscillation along the apse line that mimics Mercury's varying deferent distance without any non-uniform motion.

Ptolemy confirms: R☿ epicycle = 22;30 parts (Almagest IX.9, p.458). Al-Shatir preserves this radius. The Tusi outer radius≈3;0 parts, each distance between centres = 3;0 (Almagest IX.9). ✓

Why the epicycle is so large

In the geocentric system, Venus never strays far from the Sun because the primary epicycle centre is locked to the solar mean longitude. The epicycle itself is large (R₂≈43) — nearly as large as the deferent (R₁=60) — because Venus's maximum elongation of ~47° requires arcsin(43/60) ≈ 46°. Ptolemy established this in Almagest X.2 from observed elongations. Al-Shatir preserved the radius but eliminated the equant by adding a small correction epicycle.

Almagest confirmation: Ptolemy derives Venus epicycle radius = 43;10 parts where deferent = 60 (Almagest X.2). Greatest morning elongation 43°, evening 48° (Almagest X.3, from observations 134 CE and 140 CE). Max elongation = arcsin(43.167/60) = 45.9° ≈ average of 43° and 48°. ✓

How the retrograde emerges

When Mars approaches opposition (M♂≈180°), the primary arm points outward from Earth and the secondary arm (−M♂≈180°) also points outward but has magnitude R₃=7.117 — this creates maximum elongation from the deferent path. As M♂ advances past 180°, the secondary arm swings around and briefly drags Mars backward against the stars (retrograde). The R₃ counter-rotation is the sole source of the retrograde loop in al-Shatir's model.

Retrograde condition: Retrograde occurs when |dλ/dt| < 0, which requires the transverse velocity of the secondary arm to exceed the forward motion. For Mars: R₃·ωM = 7.117 × 0.461629 ≈ 3.29 °/day must briefly exceed the forward rate. This occurs for ~73 days near opposition, matching observed Mars retrograde duration. ✓

Structure matches Mars exactly

All three superior planets (Mars, Jupiter, Saturn) use the identical double-epicycle structure: α₁=mean longitude (forward), α₂=mean anomaly (forward), α₃=−mean anomaly (reverse). Only the radii differ. The primary (R₂) is always small and absorbs the equant displacement; the secondary (R₃) produces the retrograde by swinging backward at opposition. For Saturn, 29 years of slow deferent motion means its position changes by only ~12.2° per year — over our 300-year span, it completes just ~10 full circuits.

II. What Cannot Be Done with Circle Geometry Alone

Planetary latitude — The ecliptic longitude computed by the double-epicycle chain is a 2D quantity on the plane of the ecliptic. All seven bodies actually move slightly above and below this plane. The Moon tilts ~5°, Venus ~3.4°, Mercury ~7°, superior planets ~1–2.5°. Computing these requires a separate inclined-plane model with its own nodal regression rate. This is not circle geometry in the ecliptic plane — it requires a 3D spherical construction. Al-Shatir had such a model; we have not implemented it here.
Apogee precession — The solar apogee (and all planetary apse lines) precess slowly — approximately 1.4°/century for the Sun, similar rates for the planets. This is not generated by the uniform circular motions in the model; it must be imposed as a secular correction applied to the apogee longitude. Without it, longitude errors grow to ~4° over the full 300-year span. We apply a linear apogee correction for the Sun only.
The Tusi couple's limiting case — The Tusi couple (inner circle radius r rolling inside outer radius 2r) is technically circle geometry, but it is a degenerate case: a circle of zero radius in the limit, tracing a diameter. We implement it as two arms with a −2ω counter-rotation relationship, which is mathematically equivalent for longitude computation. This is the most sophisticated element in the entire system.
Higher-order inequalities — The Moon has additional perturbations (variation, annual equation, parallactic inequality) beyond what the double-epicycle captures. Jupiter and Saturn have mutual gravitational perturbations that accumulate over decades. These require terms beyond simple circular motion and were not known to al-Shatir.
